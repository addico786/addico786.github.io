Search “website development cost in India” and the first page is eight agency
blogs. Read four of them and you will notice something: the number is always a
range wide enough to fit whatever you were going to be quoted anyway, and the
page always ends with a contact form.

I am a freelancer. I have no sales team to feed, so I can just tell you the
numbers.

## What things actually cost

These are the real 2026 market ranges, not my quote sheet. Where two figures
sit far apart, the difference is almost always scope, not skill.

| What you want | Freelancer | Agency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Portfolio or personal site | ₹10,000 – ₹60,000 | ₹60,000 – ₹1,50,000 |
| Small business site (home, services, about, contact) | ₹15,000 – ₹80,000 | ₹80,000 – ₹3,00,000 |
| Business site with CMS, 5–15 pages | ₹25,000 – ₹1,50,000 | ₹1,50,000 – ₹4,00,000 |
| Basic store (Shopify or WooCommerce) | ₹50,000 – ₹1,50,000 | ₹1,50,000 – ₹5,00,000 |
| Store with custom filtering, multi-vendor, inventory | ₹3,00,000+ | ₹3,00,000 – ₹10,00,000 |

By the hour, Indian freelancers sit at roughly **₹500 – ₹2,000**, with
mid-level people clustering around ₹1,200 – ₹2,000. Agencies bill
₹2,000 – ₹3,000 for the same work, because you are also paying for the project
manager, the QA pass and the account handler.

> If a quote for a five-page business site lands above ₹3,00,000, that is not a
> price for a website. That is a price for a relationship, and you should decide
> deliberately whether you want one.

## What the agency markup actually buys

I want to be fair here, because the honest answer is “sometimes quite a lot.”

You are paying for **redundancy**. If I get typhoid, your project stops. If an
agency's developer gets typhoid, someone else picks it up on Monday. For a site
that has a launch date tied to a funding round or a trade show, that is worth
real money.

You are paying for **process** — structured revision rounds, a QA pass by
someone who did not write the code, and a contract with a support window
attached to it.

You are **not** paying for better code. The 2026 freelance market has split
hard: AI tooling has genuinely wiped out the bottom tier — anyone whose only
skill was assembling a stock WordPress theme is finding it very hard to charge
for that now — while rates for specialists have gone up, not down. The
person writing your code is as likely to be excellent at ₹40,000 as at
₹2,00,000. What changes is what surrounds them.

## The costs that are not in the quote

This is where most first-time buyers get surprised, so budget for it up front:

- **Domain** — ₹99 to ₹2,500 a year. Buy it in your own name, on your own
  account. This matters more than it sounds like it does.
- **Hosting** — anywhere from ₹69 a month to ₹50,000 a year. A static
  marketing site can genuinely run free on Cloudflare Pages or Netlify. If
  someone quotes you ₹15,000 a year for hosting a five-page brochure site, ask
  what it is running on.
- **SSL** — free. If it is a line item, question it.
- **Maintenance and SEO** — ₹1,000 to ₹50,000 a month, and the honest range for
  a small business is the bottom third of that.

One thing worth paying attention to: ask where the site will be hosted, and
whether you are sharing a server with a few hundred other sites. Cheap shared
hosting is one of the most common reasons a site that was “fast when we
launched” is slow eighteen months later.

## What makes the number go up

In rough order of how much they move the price:

1. **Custom design versus a customised template.** This is the single biggest
   lever, and often the least important to your customers.
2. **Content.** If you have not written the words, someone has to, and writing
   twelve pages of real copy is a bigger job than building them.
3. **Integrations.** Payments, CRM, booking, inventory — each one is a system
   that can break independently.
4. **Animation and interaction work.** Genuinely expensive to do well, and
   genuinely worth nothing if it makes the site slow on a phone.
5. **Number of pages.** Least important. Pages are cheap; the first one is
   expensive.

## What I would tell you to do

Buy the smallest thing that can prove the business case. Five pages, fast, with
real content and working analytics, for ₹40,000 – ₹80,000. Run it for three
months. Then spend the money on whatever the data says is missing, instead of
on what a mockup made you want in month one.

Three questions worth asking whoever you hire, freelancer or agency:

- **Do I own everything?** Domain, hosting account, code, content. In my
  name, on my accounts, from day one.
- **Show me how I change a price on the site.** If nobody can walk you through
  it in five minutes, you are buying a dependency, not a website.
- **Who did the design, and who wrote the code?** Portfolios routinely mix
  the two. Ask which half is theirs.

If the answers are good, the price is probably fine.
